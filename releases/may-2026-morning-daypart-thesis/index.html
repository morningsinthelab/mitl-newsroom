The Morning Daypart Has Moved to YouTube. Nobody Is Programming for It. — Mornings in the Lab Files Thesis on the Biggest Underserved Audience in Streaming

TORONTO, ONTARIO, CANADA — May 2026 — MiTL Studio, the live conversation studio behind Mornings in the Lab, today released its first formal thesis on the structural opportunity reshaping morning television: the morning daypart has migrated to YouTube and streaming, but virtually no one is programming for it as a daily live destination. While legacy network morning shows have lost nearly half their viewers over the past decade, the audience has not disappeared — it has moved, and it is being served almost nothing built for it.

The thesis arrives at a moment of crisis for traditional morning television. According to Forbes, “Today” has lost 45% of its viewership over the comparable period (from 4.71 million to 2.6 million viewers), and “CBS Mornings” has dropped 47% (Forbes, April 22, 2026). At the same time, YouTube has captured a structural lead in the streaming category, accounting for 12.6% of all time spent streaming in the US — more than any other streamer (Nielsen via eMarketer) — and pulling 47% of total time spent streaming with YouTube included (Nielsen, The Gauge, January 2026).

The migration is not theoretical. According to a January 2026 Morning Consult brand study, YouTube TV showed a “very strong Mental Advantage (+11) on catching morning news shows — one of the clearest signals in the entire matrix.” Netflix scored -12, Prime -7, and Max -7 on the same metric (Morning Consult). Morning is daily, habitual, and time-specific. It is structurally incompatible with binge-first platforms.

“The networks are losing the morning because they keep producing for a 1995 viewer who doesn’t exist anymore. There are 165 million monthly podcast listeners in the US. There are tens of millions of adults watching YouTube on their TV before work. And the only thing being made for them in the morning daypart is the same three network shows that have lost half their audience. We are not waiting for the networks to figure it out. We are building the show that should already exist.”

— Keith Bilous, Founder, MiTL Studio

The Numbers Behind the Thesis

  • Morning network television has lost ~46% of viewers across “Today” and “CBS Mornings” over the past decade (Forbes).
  • Streaming now accounts for 66.7% of ad-supported TV time among 18–49-year-olds (Nielsen, March 2026).
  • 92% of US adults use at least one streaming service, and 55% of consumers are streaming-only with no cable (CableTV.com State of TV 2026).
  • YouTube holds the strongest Mental Advantage (+11) for catching morning news, the clearest single signal in Morning Consult’s brand-association matrix (Morning Consult).
  • 165 million monthly US podcast listeners — 55% of the 12+ population — consume podcasts regularly, with YouTube now commanding 25%+ of US podcast consumption (Digital Applied / Edison Research Infinite Dial 2026).
  • Video-first interview podcasts grew 34% year-over-year — the fastest-growing podcast format (Digital Applied).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Morning Daypart Thesis?

The thesis argues that the morning television audience has migrated to YouTube and streaming, but no one is programming a daily live morning show built natively for that environment. Network morning TV has lost nearly half its viewers over the past decade, while YouTube now commands the largest share of streaming time and the strongest brand association for morning news. Mornings in the Lab is the operating proof of what a YouTube-native morning show looks like.

Why is this a structural opportunity and not just a content gap?

Morning is daily, habitual, and time-specific. It rewards format consistency, live presence, and platform fit. Binge-driven platforms like Netflix and Prime are mismatched to the daypart. YouTube, with its TV-first viewing patterns and the strongest brand association for morning news, is the only platform structurally suited for it — and almost no one is producing daily live shows built natively for it.
